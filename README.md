## The problem

Accounting firms that serve startups and SMBs still rebuild the same monthly package for lenders, boards, and buyers. The layout, logo, disclaimer, and KPI header get copied into a new spreadsheet for every client, every month. That work does not scale, and it is exactly the job firm report templates are meant to take off a reviewer's plate.

Kick is an AI bookkeeping and accounting platform. In the six weeks before this kit was assembled, it shipped the workflow those firms already sell.

## Why accounting firms, why now

On November 6, 2026 Kick launched a [report editor with custom reports and firm templates](https://www.kick.co/changelog/report-editor). A firm can save wording, number formats, a logo, a disclaimer, or a Draft watermark once, then apply that standard across clients, with overrides where one entity needs to differ. Kick's own changelog says an assistant connected through Kick MCP can act on a prompt such as "Create a lender-ready template and assign it to all my clients".

That launch sits on a firm-facing run already in market, all listed on the [Kick changelog](https://www.kick.co/changelog):

- October 30, 2026: checks and deposits extracted from paperwork, free for all users
- October 16, 2026: Chart of Accounts editor and CoA templates for accountants
- October 9, 2026: continuous revenue recognition
- October 1, 2026: free invoicing, including accountant-oriented line-level coding
- September 24, 2026: automated multi-entity consolidation and an Ownership Canvas

Kick already sells to firms. The [accountants page](https://www.kick.co/accountants) offers a free firm account, client management, group AI training, and a separate AI-Native Program. Kick's docs describe a [Partner Program](https://docs.kick.co/referrals-and-partnerships/partner-program) with multi-entity support, role-based client access, and client referrals, and [per-client accountant pricing](https://docs.kick.co/accountants/reference/plan-comparison-guide) plus a Kick Accountant AI Program with custom pricing and lead generation.

This kit turns that moment into something a sales leader can use: a scored list of real firms, a sample lender-ready P&L, and a short outbound sequence.

## What is in the workbook

Open `Kick_Accounting_Firm_GTM_Kit.xlsx`. There are four tabs.

### Firm Leads

Twenty-three U.S. accounting firms, outsourced accounting shops, and fractional-CFO practices. Each row was checked against a page we opened on November 9, 2026. Known Kick customers named on Kick's accountants page were left off, so this is a prospecting set, not a customer list.

Each firm has five rubric points, scored 0 or 1:

1. Client fit: the firm's own site says it serves startups, SMBs, ecommerce brands, or growing privately held companies.
2. Service fit: it offers monthly bookkeeping or accounting, and/or fractional CFO or outsourced controller work.
3. Delivery fit: it mentions multi-client or multi-entity delivery, standardized reporting, or work in QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite, or Sage.
4. Why-now: a current public signal on a fetched page, such as a 2025 or 2026 announcement, a growth claim, hiring or expansion, a new service, or an explicit tech-stack or lender-reporting mention.
5. Scale: it published a team size of roughly 5 to 200 people, or it clearly describes itself as a specialist or boutique firm rather than a Big 4 or national top-50 generalist.

The five blue weight cells at the top default to 1. **Weighted Score** is a `SUMPRODUCT` of those weights and the five rubric points. **Rank** is a `RANK` formula (1 is best). If you leave the weights at 1, the score matches the original 1 to 5 tally. Raise Why-now to 2 if you want recent public signals to count more. Set a weight to 0 to ignore that criterion. Headers are frozen, the table has an autofilter, and scores are conditionally formatted.

### Sample Lender P&L

A fictional company, Harborline Robotics LLC, clearly marked **SAMPLE DATA**. Monthly columns run from July 2026 through October 2026. Blue cells are inputs. Black cells are formulas for gross profit, gross margin, total operating expenses, EBITDA, net income, debt service (interest plus principal), DSCR, net cash flow, and cash runway. This is not a real client and not a Kick product screen. It shows how a firm could set up a lender-ready template after the November 6 launch, including branding placeholders and a sample MCP prompt.

### Outbound Sequence

Three emails and one LinkedIn touch, with day and channel, written for Kick's new sales leader to adapt. Nothing in this sequence has been sent. It cites only launches verified on Kick's public changelog and docs.

### Sources

Every Kick changelog and docs URL that was opened while building the kit, plus Anvik's LinkedIn.

## How to use the sheet

1. Read the weight note at the top of Firm Leads, then edit the blue weight cells if your ranking priorities differ.
2. Filter by segment or sort by Weighted Score using the header dropdowns.
3. Open the Source link before any outreach so you only cite a page that still says what we recorded.
4. Personalize the LinkedIn note from the Why-now signal and Suggested angle columns.
5. Use the Sample Lender P&L as a walkthrough, not as a client deliverable. Change the blue inputs to see DSCR and runway update.
